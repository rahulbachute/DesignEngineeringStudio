# Instructor Guide

## Objectives

- Introduce students to safety-oriented design review.
- Connect machine element design to elevator suspension systems.
- Encourage evidence-based engineering recommendations.

## Expected Answers

- Component identification should include traction motor, traction sheave, counterweight, steel wire rope, guide rail, elevator car, controller, and safety gear.
- Working principle responses should describe motor-driven sheave traction, rope movement, counterweight balancing, guided car motion, and controller coordination.
- Material recommendation should usually favor certified high-carbon steel wire rope, with corrosion and code considerations noted.
- Calculation sequence: passenger load = 10 x 75 = 750 kg; total load = 750 + 700 = 1450 kg; load per rope = 1450 / 4 = 362.5 kg before applying factor of safety and dynamic effects.

## Common Mistakes

- Ignoring car weight.
- Dividing passenger load only by number of ropes.
- Treating cost as more important than safety.
- Providing a recommendation without engineering justification.

## Discussion Points

- Why elevator design requires factor of safety.
- Difference between static load and dynamic operating load.
- Importance of certified materials and inspection.
- Role of counterweight in reducing motor effort.

## Suggested Evaluation

Use `rubric.json` for marks distribution. Award credit for correct reasoning, calculation process, and professional clarity.
