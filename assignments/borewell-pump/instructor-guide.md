# Instructor Guide: Borewell Pump Hand Lever

## Facilitation Strategy
This assignment requires students to integrate structural mechanics with human factors engineering. Instructors should encourage students to not only focus on the mathematical calculations of the bending moment and stress, but also critically evaluate whether the required effort is comfortable for a human operator.

### Key Concepts to Emphasize
- **First-Class Lever Mechanism:** Explain the relationship between the effort arm, load arm, and mechanical advantage. 
- **Ergonomics vs. Strength:** A lever might be structurally safe (high factor of safety) but ergonomically poor (requires too much force). Students must balance both.
- **Factor of Safety (FoS):** Emphasize that FoS is calculated against Yield Strength (Syt) for static loading of ductile materials.

## Expected Misconceptions
- **Incorrect Load Path:** Students may confuse the effort arm and the load arm on the free body diagram. The effort arm is from the fulcrum to the handle grip, and the load arm is from the fulcrum to the pump rod connection.
- **Ignoring Ergonomics:** Students might approve a design simply because the FoS > 1, failing to recognize that a 500 N effort requirement is practically impossible for repeated human use.
- **Units and Conversions:** Ensure students convert dimensions (e.g., from meters to millimeters) correctly before calculating stress and moments.

## Review Notes
When reviewing student submissions, look for strong engineering justification in the "Design Improvement" and "Engineering Decision" sections. A good submission will explicitly state that the lever length should be increased to reduce human effort, while verifying the cross-section can handle the increased bending moment.
