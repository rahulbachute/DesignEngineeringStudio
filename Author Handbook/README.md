# DES Author Handbook

Design Engineering Studio (DES) Version 1.0 is a frozen software platform. This handbook supports faculty authors who create Engineering Challenges for that platform without changing the platform architecture, interface, scripts, schema, or folder conventions.

## Purpose

DES challenges convert engineering syllabus topics into authentic design decisions. A good challenge asks students to understand a real system, identify constraints, apply engineering science, make justified choices, test assumptions, and communicate evidence.

The authoring goal is not to produce a long assignment. The goal is to create a structured engineering experience that can be run, assessed, reviewed, improved, and reused.

## Educational Philosophy

DES is built on active, evidence-based engineering learning. Students should not only remember a formula; they should experience why the formula matters, where it applies, what assumptions control it, and how design judgement affects safety, economy, manufacturability, and performance.

Each challenge should include:

- A realistic context that gives purpose to the work.
- A clear engineering problem with measurable constraints.
- Activities that move from observation to analysis to decision.
- Interactive components that make students test ideas, not merely read content.
- Assessment evidence that shows what students can actually do.
- Reflection that helps students consolidate professional judgement.

## DES Design Principles

1. Start from an engineering situation, not from a topic title.
2. Make the student act like an engineer: inspect, estimate, model, calculate, compare, decide, document.
3. Keep calculations connected to physical meaning.
4. Make assumptions explicit.
5. Assess process and justification, not only final numerical answers.
6. Use interactive components only when they improve learning.
7. Align every challenge with CO, PO, PSO, WK, Bloom level, and CCE requirements.
8. Keep content compatible with the frozen DES platform.

## Outcome-Based Education (OBE)

OBE begins with intended learning outcomes and designs learning evidence around them. In DES, every challenge must identify the Course Outcomes (COs) it supports and provide activities that generate observable evidence.

When authoring, ask:

- What should the student be able to do after this challenge?
- Which CO is being developed or assessed?
- What response, calculation, model, comparison, or reflection will prove it?
- Does the rubric directly evaluate that evidence?

## National Board of Accreditation (NBA)

NBA emphasizes outcome attainment, continuous improvement, professional competencies, and documented assessment. DES challenges support NBA readiness by making challenge objectives, mappings, rubrics, evidence, and review records explicit.

A challenge should help demonstrate:

- CO attainment through mapped activities and rubrics.
- PO and PSO relevance through engineering tasks.
- Graduate attribute development through design, analysis, communication, ethics, sustainability, teamwork, and lifelong learning prompts where appropriate.
- Continuous improvement through faculty review, student pilot feedback, and revision notes.

## National Education Policy 2020 (NEP 2020)

NEP 2020 encourages multidisciplinary, experiential, flexible, skill-oriented, and learner-centered education. DES supports these priorities by using authentic problems, digital interaction, design thinking, and reflective learning.

Authors should include opportunities for:

- Conceptual understanding instead of rote problem solving.
- Hands-on or simulation-based exploration.
- Contextual connection with industry, society, safety, sustainability, and local needs.
- Creativity in solution choice within engineering constraints.
- Self-reflection and iterative improvement.

## Continuous and Comprehensive Evaluation (CCE)

CCE treats assessment as an ongoing learning process. DES challenges should provide multiple evidence points across the challenge, such as observations, intermediate calculations, interactive responses, design justification, notebook entries, and reflection.

CCE marks must be linked to clear criteria. Avoid assigning marks only to completion. Award marks for engineering reasoning, accuracy, documentation, safety awareness, assumptions, and reflection.

## Experiential Learning

Experiential learning follows a cycle: experience, reflect, conceptualize, and apply. DES challenges should let students encounter a practical situation, work through engineering decisions, reflect on the result, and generalize the learning.

Use experiential prompts such as:

- What did you observe?
- What assumption did you make?
- What would change in a real installation?
- How would a failure appear in service?
- What would you check before approving the design?

## Project-Based Learning

Project-Based Learning organizes learning around a meaningful product, decision, or deliverable. In DES, the deliverable may be a design verification, engineering notebook, calculation sheet, recommendation memo, comparison table, inspection report, or design improvement proposal.

The project should have:

- A driving engineering question.
- A defined stakeholder or industry situation.
- Technical constraints.
- Student decision making.
- A final deliverable assessed through a rubric.

## Sections of an Engineering Challenge

### Metadata

Metadata identifies, maps, and controls the challenge. It tells the platform and reviewers what the challenge is, where it belongs, how difficult it is, how it will be assessed, and who approved it.

### Challenge Title

The title should be short, specific, and engineering-centered. It should name the system and the design or verification action.

Example: `Motorcycle Side Stand Load Verification`.

### Industry Scenario

The industry scenario gives students a realistic reason to solve the problem. It should describe the product, operating condition, stakeholder concern, constraint, or failure risk.

### Project Charter

The charter defines the engineering mission. It states the problem, client or stakeholder, objectives, constraints, assumptions, deliverables, success criteria, and assessment evidence.

### Engineering Background

This section gives the minimum theory needed to work responsibly. It should include concepts, formulas, symbols, units, assumptions, limits of validity, and links to course content.

### Learning Outcomes

Learning outcomes describe what students should be able to do. They must align with COs and be assessable through student work.

### Engineering Activities

Activities are the core learning path. They should be sequenced from understanding the system to applying calculations, interpreting results, making decisions, and documenting evidence.

### Interactive Components

Interactive components are the DES platform elements students use to explore, calculate, label, compare, select, or submit. Each component must have a learning purpose and map to an activity.

### Engineering Tips

Tips provide helpful reminders without giving away the solution. They should guide students toward correct assumptions, unit discipline, diagram interpretation, and engineering checks.

### Common Mistakes

This section warns against predictable errors such as unit mismatch, wrong free-body diagram, ignoring factor of safety, assuming static conditions where dynamic loads matter, or reporting unsupported conclusions.

### Think Like an Engineer

This section prompts judgement. It asks students to consider safety, manufacturability, cost, reliability, maintenance, ethics, sustainability, and real-world consequences.

### Reflection

Reflection helps students explain what they learned, what assumptions mattered, how their solution might change in practice, and how they would improve their work.

### Faculty Notes

Faculty notes support delivery. They may include expected difficulty, facilitation tips, misconception alerts, alternate discussion prompts, required preparation, and review comments.

### Assessment Rubric

The rubric converts learning evidence into marks. It should include criteria, performance levels, marks, CO or PO links, and expectations for technical accuracy and reasoning.

### Report or Engineering Notebook

The notebook template defines what students submit. It should capture problem understanding, assumptions, diagrams, calculations, interactive observations, design decisions, conclusions, and reflection.

### Version and Review History

Version history documents authorship, review, pilot use, changes, and approval status. It supports maintainability and quality control.

## Authoring Rule

DES Version 1.0 software is frozen. Faculty authors must create and revise challenge content only. Do not change platform code, JavaScript, CSS, HTML, JSON schema, namespace conventions, Google Sheets integration, folder structure, challenge runner, or submission engine.
