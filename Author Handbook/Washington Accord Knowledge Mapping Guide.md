# Washington Accord Knowledge Mapping Guide

This guide helps DES authors map Engineering Challenges to the Washington Accord knowledge profile. Use WK mapping to show the level and type of engineering knowledge demonstrated by the student.

## WK1: Natural Sciences

Knowledge of mathematics, natural science, and engineering fundamentals.

Evidence examples:

- Student applies physics or mechanics principles.
- Student uses mathematical relationships correctly.
- Student interprets units, dimensions, and physical quantities.

## WK2: Conceptually-Based Mathematics, Numerical Analysis, Statistics, and Computing

Knowledge of mathematics, numerical methods, statistics, and computing relevant to engineering.

Evidence examples:

- Student performs calculation using equations or numerical methods.
- Student uses tabulated data, interpolation, or computation.
- Student checks sensitivity of a design variable.

## WK3: Engineering Fundamentals

Systematic, theory-based understanding of core engineering principles.

Evidence examples:

- Student constructs a free-body diagram.
- Student applies stress, strain, torque, bending, fatigue, or design principles.
- Student connects formula selection to engineering assumptions.

## WK4: Specialist Engineering Knowledge

Specialist knowledge in a specific engineering discipline.

Evidence examples:

- Student applies machine design criteria.
- Student selects or verifies a component for a specific mechanical function.
- Student uses discipline-specific terminology and design practice.

## WK5: Engineering Design

Knowledge that supports engineering design, including constraints, standards, and user needs.

Evidence examples:

- Student compares design alternatives.
- Student selects dimensions, materials, or safety factors.
- Student justifies a design choice against constraints.

## WK6: Engineering Practice

Knowledge of engineering tools, methods, standards, codes, procedures, and professional practice.

Evidence examples:

- Student uses a design handbook, standard, or manufacturer data.
- Student documents assumptions and checks.
- Student considers manufacturability, assembly, maintenance, or inspection.

## WK7: Role of Engineering in Society

Knowledge of societal, health, safety, legal, cultural, and environmental responsibilities.

Evidence examples:

- Student discusses consequences of component failure.
- Student considers user safety and public risk.
- Student identifies environmental or regulatory implications.

## WK8: Research Literature and Knowledge Development

Engagement with current knowledge, literature, data, or investigation.

Evidence examples:

- Student refers to a data sheet, design guide, standard, paper, or credible technical source.
- Student compares observed results with reference values.
- Student identifies the need for further testing or validation.

## WK9: Ethics, Inclusive Practice, and Sustainable Development

Knowledge of ethical responsibilities, inclusive engineering practice, sustainability, and professional conduct.

Evidence examples:

- Student identifies ethical risk in approving an unsafe design.
- Student considers accessibility, user diversity, or maintainability.
- Student evaluates sustainability, material use, repairability, or lifecycle impact.

## Mapping Guidance

- Map only WKs that are genuinely evidenced.
- Do not over-map a challenge to every WK.
- For each mapped WK, identify where the student produces evidence.
- Align mapped WKs with rubric criteria.
- Review WK mapping after pilot use to confirm that student submissions actually demonstrate the mapped knowledge.
